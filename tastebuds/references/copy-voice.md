# Copy and voice

## Claim discipline

**Never print a number you have not verified against a source.**

The failure mode is specific and common: a plausible figure appears in a draft,
survives review because it sounds right, and ships as a factual claim on a
public page. Latency figures, market share, user counts, throughput, "10x
faster". Those are the ones that get invented.

Before any number ships, find where it comes from. If no source states it:

- **Cut it.** Usually the sentence is stronger without.
- **Or hedge it honestly.** "Around", "more than", a range. Hedged and true
  beats precise and invented.
- **Never** reconstruct a number by reasoning from adjacent facts and present
  the result as measured.

The same applies to mechanism claims. If the interaction is a right-click, do
not write "click the icon" because it reads better.

Anything time-bound (a campaign, a date, a promotional rate) needs an expiry
noted somewhere, because it becomes a false claim on a known date.

## Talk about what you do

The most common voice failure in a product built on a platform, marketplace, or
supplier is spending the page praising the thing underneath. It reads as
derivative and tells the reader nothing about why to choose you.

Name the dependency once, factually, where it builds trust. Spend the rest of
the page on what you built: what it does, what it makes possible, what changes
for the reader.

The test: could a competitor on the same platform publish this page unchanged?
If yes, it is about the platform, not the product.

## Headings that say something

A heading should carry information, not label a category.

- Weak: "Powerful Features", "Built for Speed", "Our Approach"
- Better: state the specific thing. "It crosses the spread on your terms."
  "Close the browser and it keeps running."

Specificity is what makes a page feel like it was written by someone who knows
the product. Category labels are what a template generates.

Sentence case for headings unless the brand demands otherwise. Full stops on
headings are a style choice: pick one and apply it consistently, including in
places the tagline appears alone.

## Mechanics

- **No em-dashes.** Use a full stop, a comma, or restructure. This is absolute
  for visible copy.
- **Second person.** "You" beats "users" or "traders" or "customers".
- **Active voice.** "The app schedules the work" not "the work is scheduled".
- **One idea per sentence.** Long sentences in body copy at small sizes are
  where readers leave.
- **Numerals for numbers** in interface and marketing copy.
- **Consistent terminology.** One name per concept across the entire product and
  site. Synonyms read as different things.

## Eyebrows and labels

Uppercase eyebrows are structural, not decorative: they tell the reader where
they are. One or two words, naming the section's subject.

They should not repeat the heading, and they should not be cute. If the heading
is "It crosses the spread on your terms", the eyebrow is "Escalation", not
"Smart execution".

## Length

Marketing body copy: two to four sentences per block. If a block needs more,
it wants to be two blocks or a different format.

Write the long version first, then cut. The cut version is almost always the
one that ships, and it is much easier to cut than to pad.
